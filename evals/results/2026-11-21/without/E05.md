I can build it, but I would push back on the pie chart: with 9 categories it is a poor choice, and I'd recommend a different visual unless you have a specific reason for the pie.

**Why not a pie with 9 slices**
- People compare angles and areas poorly, so slices of similar size (say 8% vs 10%) are impossible to rank by eye.
- 9 slices need 9 distinguishable colors and a legend, which forces the eye to jump back and forth. Small slices get cramped labels.
- A pie only works for a few parts (2 to 5) where one slice clearly dominates.

**What I recommend instead**
1. **Sorted horizontal bar chart** (best option): categories on the axis sorted by revenue descending, data labels showing revenue share (% of total). Every category is readable and ranked at a glance.
2. If you need the "part of whole" feeling: a **100% stacked single bar**, or a **treemap**, or a **donut with the top 4 or 5 categories plus "Others"** (grouping the long tail into one slice).
3. If the share is the message, bars with the `% of total` label work well and you can add a cumulative line (Pareto) if you want to show concentration.

If you still want the pie, I'll make it readable: sort slices by value, group the smallest ones into "Others" (e.g. under 3%), use percentage labels outside, and a single-hue or limited palette.

**The measure I would use (works for any of the visuals)**
```DAX
Receita = SUM ( Vendas[Receita] )

Receita % Total =
DIVIDE ( [Receita], CALCULATE ( [Receita], ALLSELECTED ( Produto[Categoria] ) ) )
```
`ALLSELECTED` keeps the share consistent with the slicers/page filters the user applies, instead of always being relative to the whole dataset (use `ALL` if you want the fixed total).
